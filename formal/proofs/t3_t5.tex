\section*{T3 -- Conditional upper bounds on suppression}
With a trusted current clock and mandatory heartbeats, deleting the last
sealed period causes a stale-tip verdict. An internal deletion of a sealed
period creates a period gap or breaks checkpoint links. Changing sealed bytes
while preserving period identity requires a signature or Merkle forgery
(T1--T2). If a later cosigned or anchored checkpoint covers the removed
records, the independent holder has evidence of their prior existence.

\textbf{Counterexample without freshness:} Removing a final sealed
checkpoint together with all its events leaves a signature-valid prefix;
this may be accepted without a time policy or an external tip. Therefore the
$W_{\mathrm{silent}}$ upper bound is false in that weaker model.

\section*{T5 -- Lower bounds (open)}
A collector that has not emitted a message depending on an event has no
externally witnessed evidence of that event. An attacker that sees the
unsealed mutable state can attempt to simulate the collector on a trace
where the event never arrived. The distributions are not necessarily
identical if input acknowledgments or third-party telemetry leak event
existence.
