\section*{T4 -- First-divergence same-period argument}
Take two non-prefix-consistent, contiguous signed checkpoint histories of
the same collector and epoch with the same authenticated genesis. At their
first differing index $p$, they have distinct checkpoint digests for period
$p$, unless a digest collision occurs. If both are accepted under one
schedule, there are two signatures authorizing the distinct digests with the
schedule's period-$p$ key. Therefore the pair is candidate fraud evidence.

\section*{T8 -- Soundness of publicly verified equivocation evidence}
Two valid low-$s$ secp256k1 signatures on distinct digests, tied by a single
schedule leaf and period index, cannot be emitted by an honest collector that
signs at most one digest per period, absent a signature forgery or crash
non-equivocation failure. The on-chain verifier must reject a high-$s$
signature and mismatched collector, period, epoch, or schedule root.
